NEIGHBORHOODS & PLACE

Get oriented without flattening the city.

Millbrae has named streets, school communities, commercial corridors, hillside pockets, park systems, and planning areas that overlap—but they are not all the same thing.

Important distinction: This is an orientation guide, not a legal boundary map. The City’s General Plan and specific plans are the authoritative planning sources; school assignments, election districts, and service areas can use different boundaries.

PLACE NOTES

Four ways residents encounter Millbrae.

These are useful lenses for getting oriented, not official neighborhood boundaries.

STATION & EL CAMINO

Movement, services, and the old rail story.

The station and El Camino Real corridor bring together regional transit, hotels, restaurants, civic connections, and the historic depot. This is the clearest lens for understanding why Millbrae feels both local and connected to the wider Peninsula.

Plan from Millbrae Station →

WESTERN HILLSIDES

Residential streets shaped by slope and view.

General Plan labels such as Vallejo, Skyline, Crestview, Sycamore, Fernwood, Ridgewood, Santa Teresa, and San Anselmo help readers locate the hillside geography. Street names and formal land-use maps are more reliable than assuming one universal neighborhood label.

SCHOOL COMMUNITIES

Schools create everyday social geography.

Green Hills, Meadows, Spring Valley, Taylor, and the other district schools are community anchors, but attendance areas can differ from informal neighborhood names. Use the district’s assigned-school information when the question affects enrollment.
