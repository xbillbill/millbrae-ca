INTEREST STORY · BUSINESSES · PLACE

Local businesses are part of Millbrae’s map.

A storefront is more than a category in a directory. It sits in a corridor, serves a particular need, connects to a neighborhood, and changes over time. A useful local guide keeps those layers visible.

Story method: City economic-development and business resources explain the public context. Business websites and owner-maintained listings answer current offerings. This story adds orientation without turning visibility into a ranking.

01 · The commercial map

Businesses live in corridors, not only categories.

Broadway and downtown, El Camino Real, Millbrae Avenue, and the station-and-SFO area place businesses in different everyday contexts. The same category can mean a different trip depending on whether someone is walking from a neighborhood, transferring at the station, serving airport travelers, or looking for a recurring local service.

The City’s economic-development and map resources provide public context; they do not replace a business’s own current hours, menu, inventory, or service area.

03 · A wider customer geography

Transit changes who can reach a local place.

Millbrae Station brings together BART, Caltrain, SamTrans, highways, hotels, and access to SFO. That makes the business map larger than the city’s residential blocks—but connection is context, not a promise about any individual business’s performance.

For a visitor, “near the station” may mean a walk, a transfer, a curb, or a short drive. For a resident, the more important question may be repeat access, neighborhood fit, or a service that solves an everyday problem.

04 · Use a listing carefully

Current business information has a short shelf life.

Choose the fit

Start with the need, category, location, access, and any practical constraint that matters to your decision.

Check the source

Open the direct business website or call. Confirm hours, holiday changes, prices, reservations, accessibility, and availability.

Read the label

Community-submitted, editorial, official, and paid information have different responsibilities. Do not treat them as interchangeable evidence.

Share a correction

A useful correction includes the business, place, date, changed detail, and an original source that can be checked.

For listing changes, use the self-service listing flow. For the editorial standard and source roles, see the source registry.
