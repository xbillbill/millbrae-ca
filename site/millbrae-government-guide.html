THE MILLBRAE CIVIC GUIDE

How the city works—and where your voice enters.

Millbrae’s public decisions move through elected policy-makers, appointed administration, departments, commissions, meetings, and records. This guide connects those pieces so a resident can find the right door.

Checked against City sources: August 21, 2026. This snapshot follows the City Council page and the City’s May 2026 resignation notice; the County LAFCO profile still shows the pre-resignation District 3 lineup, so it is not used for current officeholder status.

The basic structure

Council sets policy. The City Manager runs the organization.

Millbrae uses a Council–Manager form of government. The five-member City Council is the legislative body: it adopts ordinances and resolutions, approves the budget, sets priorities, and makes final decisions on major city matters. The City Manager carries out those policies and oversees day-to-day municipal operations.

City Council

Five district-elected members share equal voting power on Council items. The Council annually chooses a mayor and vice mayor from among its members.

Where residents participate

There is more than one civic doorway.

Before a meeting

Check the City calendar and agenda center. Read the staff report or attached documents before deciding what question you want answered.

At a meeting

Attend a Council, commission, or committee meeting and follow the City’s public-comment instructions. Accessibility and language assistance can be arranged through the City Clerk.

Between meetings

Use Millbrae Works for non-emergency service requests, the public-records pathway for documents, and department contacts for operational questions.

Beyond Council

Planning, parks and recreation, cultural arts, youth, senior, economic-development, and oversight committees let residents contribute subject-matter experience.
