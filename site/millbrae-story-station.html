INTEREST STORY · RAIL · PLACE

One station, many scales.

Millbrae Station is a practical place to catch a train. It is also a useful way to understand the city: a small Peninsula municipality connected to regional rail, county bus service, an international airport, and the everyday life around a downtown.

Story method: The agency facts below are linked to the institutions that maintain them. The phrase “regional hinge” is our interpretation of what those connections mean, not an official designation.

01 · The physical place

Start at 200 North Rollins Road.

BART identifies Millbrae Station at 200 North Rollins Road and describes a cross-platform connection to Caltrain. That detail matters: the regional connection is not an abstract line on a map; it is a physical transfer point in the city.

The station area sits beside US 101 and SFO, with downtown and residential streets to the west. The result is a place where a commuter, an airport traveler, a local bus rider, and a Millbrae neighbor may share the same platform for entirely different reasons.

02 · Three systems, different jobs

“Transit” is not one thing.

The same station serves different trips and different agencies.

BART

Regional rapid transit.

BART connects Millbrae with the wider Bay Area and provides the rail path to SFO’s International Terminal station. Check BART for current schedules, fares, parking, and service alerts.

Peninsula and South Bay rail.

Caltrain uses the shared station to connect north toward San Francisco and south toward Silicon Valley and the Peninsula. Its schedules and stop information are the authority for a specific train.

03 · The airport next door

SFO changes the scale of an ordinary trip.

SFO is close enough that airport movement becomes part of Millbrae’s daily geography, but the airport and the city remain different institutions. BART’s SFO station is in the International Terminal, Level 3; the airport’s AirTrain connects that station to the domestic terminals.

That distinction is useful for planning: a Millbrae hotel, a local street, a BART platform, an AirTrain stop, and a terminal gate are connected, but each has its own operator, schedule, and failure point.

04 · Why this is a Millbrae story

The station is infrastructure—and identity.

For visitors

Millbrae can be a practical base for reaching SFO and the Peninsula without staying inside an airport terminal.

For commuters

Different rail agencies turn the station into a daily decision about direction, time, fare, and transfer.

For neighbors

Transit brings movement, but downtown, parks, schools, businesses, and civic institutions make the place a city rather than a platform.

For the future

Station-area planning has to balance regional access with local streets, housing, businesses, public space, and the airport edge.
