HISTORY STORY · NAME · RAIL

What Millbrae remembers.

A city name can be a small archive. In Millbrae, the name opens onto land, a family estate, a railroad, a depot, and the later work of becoming a city.

History method: The sequence below follows the City’s cultural-resources material and cross-checks the incorporation date against current City, County LAFCO, and court sources. Where a phrase is an interpretation of the record, it is labeled as such.

01 · Before the city

Begin with land, not a city boundary.

City cultural-resources material identifies the 1835 Mexican land grant to José Antonio Sánchez as an early chapter in the history of the land that includes present-day Millbrae. That is a different kind of fact from the later incorporation date: it describes the place’s earlier land history, not the creation of the municipal government.

Keeping those layers separate matters. A city story is not a single founding moment; it is a sequence of land, people, institutions, movement, and civic decisions that accumulate over time.

02 · The name and the landscape

“Mill brae” is a clue, not the whole answer.

The City’s historical material connects the name with the Mills estate and the rolling-hills landscape.

THE MILLS ESTATE

A name tied to a place.

The name Millbrae is associated with Darius Ogden Mills’s estate and the phrase “mill’s rolling hills.” The phrase helps describe how landscape and ownership entered the city’s later identity.

03 · Rail makes the timeline move

When the railroad arrived, the place connected outward.

The cultural-resources history identifies Southern Pacific rail service in the 1860s and the construction of a depot in 1906. The depot is now part of the Millbrae Train Museum and remains a visible reminder that transportation helped shape the city’s relationship to the Peninsula.

The railroad story is not only about trains. It is about access, movement, land use, commerce, and the way a place becomes legible to people arriving from somewhere else.

04 · Becoming a city

Incorporation adds a civic layer.

January 14, 1948

Millbrae incorporated on this date. The date appears on the City’s current facts page, San Mateo County LAFCO’s city profile, and a California appellate decision describing the incorporation certificate.

A documented discrepancy

A later City information document lists 1949. The County LAFCO and court sources support 1948, so the knowledge hub preserves the discrepancy instead of hiding it.

The civic record continues

After incorporation, budgets, elections, meetings, plans, services, and public notices become part of the city’s maintained record. That is why history and current government sources belong near each other.
