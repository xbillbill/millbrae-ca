INTEREST STORY · SCHOOLS · FAMILY LIFE

The school day is a community map.

A school is a building, but the school day also draws routes through parks, recreation, libraries, businesses, streets, and family calendars. In Millbrae, understanding that map means knowing which institution answers which question.

Story method: District and City pages maintain the current details. This story adds orientation and interpretation, while keeping enrollment, boundaries, programs, and eligibility with the responsible source.

01 · One community, several systems

“The schools” is not one institution.

Millbrae Elementary School District serves TK–8 students through Green Hills, Lomita Park, Meadows, Spring Valley, and Taylor Middle School. Mills High School is part of the San Mateo Union High School District. The City’s Recreation Department and San Mateo County Libraries add programs and public spaces that many families use alongside school.

That division is not bureaucracy for its own sake. It explains why a calendar, enrollment question, school boundary, sports program, and library event may have different owners and different update schedules.

03 · Boundaries are decisions

A school map is not the same as a neighborhood map.

Attendance areas, registration rules, grade levels, and program eligibility answer specific questions. A nearby school, an informal neighborhood name, or a remembered boundary cannot substitute for the district’s current assigned-school and enrollment sources.

Which district?

Identify TK–8 MESD or high-school SMUHSD before looking for a calendar or enrollment path.

Which address?

Use the maintained boundary and registration pages for the address, not the closest campus or a neighborhood nickname.

Which year?

Record the academic year because registration, programs, and dates are time-sensitive.

Use the MESD assigned-school source ↗ for elementary and middle-school boundary questions.
